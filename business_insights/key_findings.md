# Key Business Findings

**NYC Green Taxi Demand, Revenue & Operational Performance | January–March 2025**

## Finding 1: Taxi Route Revenue Performance

### Business Insight

East Harlem dominates the highest-revenue green taxi routes in New York City during January–March 2025.

East Harlem North to East Harlem South generated the highest recorded revenue ($88,849) from 6,187 trips. Its average revenue was $14.36 per trip, demonstrating how frequent, short-distance journeys can generate substantial revenue.

East Harlem North to Upper East Side North ranked second, generating $79,511 from 3,722 trips, with a higher average revenue of $21.36.

Airport routes demonstrated a different pattern. East Harlem North to LaGuardia Airport generated approximately $31,678 from only 639 trips, averaging $49.57 per journey.

These findings suggest two distinct revenue patterns: frequent local trips generating revenue through volume, and longer airport journeys generating more revenue per trip.

However, higher revenue per trip does not necessarily mean greater profitability.

### Strategic Recommendation

Taxi operators should investigate whether additional driver capacity is needed in East Harlem during high-demand periods.

Airport routes may offer opportunities for higher revenue per trip, particularly when drivers can minimise waiting times and empty return journeys.

Before changing driver allocation, management should analyse demand by hour and weekday, trip duration, revenue per operating hour, driver availability and vehicle operating costs.

The findings apply to NYC green taxis and should not be generalised to the entire New York City taxi market.

## Finding 2: Taxi Demand and Revenue Trends

### Business Insight

NYC green taxi demand was concentrated in weekday late afternoons during January–March 2025.

All 15 highest-ranked weekday-hour combinations occurred between 16:00 and 18:00, with 17:00 appearing in nine combinations.

The highest-demand periods were Thursday at 17:00 in January (776 trips), Friday at 17:00 in February (661 trips), and Monday at 17:00 in March (652 trips).

Revenue differences within these high-demand periods were primarily associated with trip volume, while average recorded revenue per trip remained relatively stable at approximately 25.92.

However, monthly totals are influenced by how frequently each weekday occurs. Comparing average trips per weekday occurrence provides a more reliable measure of recurring demand.

The findings suggest that weekday late afternoons are important operational periods, although the analysis covers only the highest-ranked combinations rather than every hour.

### Strategic Recommendation

Taxi operators should investigate opportunities to increase driver availability between 16:00 and 18:00 on weekdays, particularly during recurring Thursday and Friday peaks.

Shift changes and driver breaks could be scheduled outside periods of consistently high demand.

Before implementing staffing changes, management should analyse hourly demand across the full week, average trips per weekday occurrence, seasonal patterns, driver supply, waiting times and geographic concentration.

Trip duration and operating costs should also be assessed to understand revenue and profitability per operating hour.

## Finding 3: Geographic Taxi Demand Concentration

### Business Insight

NYC green taxi demand was highly concentrated in East Harlem during January–March 2025.

East Harlem North generated 33,982 trips and approximately $702,000 in recorded revenue, while East Harlem South generated 22,921 trips and approximately $486,000.

Together, these zones accounted for 41.4% of all qualifying trips. The top 15 pickup zones represented approximately 83% of network trip volume.

Despite their geographic proximity, the two East Harlem zones showed different demand patterns.

East Harlem South generated 6,634 trips during weekday afternoons (16:00–18:59), representing 28.9% of its total trips. East Harlem North recorded 4,244 trips during the same period, representing 12.5%.

Central Park also demonstrated strong weekday-afternoon concentration, with 28.7% of trips occurring during these hours.

These findings indicate that high-volume pickup zones do not necessarily have identical hourly demand patterns.

### Strategic Recommendation

Taxi operators should consider maintaining broad driver coverage in East Harlem North while investigating opportunities to increase weekday-afternoon capacity in East Harlem South and Central Park.

Forest Hills and Elmhurst may also warrant attention as important pickup locations in Queens.

Before adjusting driver allocation, management should examine existing driver supply, passenger waiting times, hourly demand, drop-off destinations and revenue per operating hour.

The analysis measures completed pickups rather than total passenger demand. Higher revenue also does not necessarily indicate greater profitability.

## Finding 4: Taxi Fare and Trip Anomalies

### Business Insight

Analysis of NYC green taxi transactions identified unusual combinations of journey duration, distance, recorded charges and average speed.

Among the 20 highest-value flagged trips, 13 were classified as long-duration journeys, six as high revenue per mile and one as high average speed.

Several records warrant investigation.

Trip 104212 recorded just 0.78 miles over approximately 11 hours, with a total charge of $473.10. This could indicate an incorrectly recorded journey or a trip that remained open longer than intended.

Multiple near-zero-distance trips recorded unusually high charges, including three transactions of exactly $301. These repeated amounts may reflect particular fare arrangements, manual adjustments or data-quality problems.

Trip 141209 recorded an average speed of approximately 168 mph, suggesting a possible timestamp or distance-recording error.

However, some flagged long-distance journeys appeared more plausible. Trips covering approximately 147–176 miles recorded average speeds and revenue-per-mile figures consistent with their longer distances.

These findings demonstrate that unusual transactions are not necessarily fraudulent or incorrect.

The analysis also has limitations: the thresholds are illustrative, the output contains only the 20 highest-value flagged trips, and each transaction is assigned only its first matching anomaly category.

### Strategic Recommendation

Taxi operators should introduce daily exception reporting to identify transactions involving unusually high charges, implausible speeds, excessive durations and extreme revenue-per-mile values.

High-value exceptions should receive priority investigation, supported by human review rather than automatic fraud classification.

Management should examine fare components, rate codes, GPS records, meter activity, driver and vehicle identifiers, and customer complaints to establish whether flagged trips were legitimate.

Multiple anomaly indicators should be retained for each transaction, allowing reviewers to identify overlapping concerns.

Over time, exception thresholds should be refined using historical trip patterns, investigation outcomes and the overall frequency of flagged transactions.
