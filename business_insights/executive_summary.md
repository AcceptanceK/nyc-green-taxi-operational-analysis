# Executive Summary

**NYC Green Taxi Demand, Revenue & Operational Performance | January–March 2025**

## Overall Performance

Analysis of NYC green taxi trips from January to March 2025 shows that activity and recorded revenue are concentrated in particular routes, locations and periods.

High-revenue local routes are driven largely by frequent short journeys, while airport trips generate higher revenue per journey but occur less frequently.

Weekday late afternoons, particularly 16:00–18:00, feature prominently among the highest-demand periods, with 17:00 consistently represented.

## Key Findings

East Harlem North and South accounted for 41.4% of qualifying trips. East Harlem also dominated the highest-revenue routes.

The two zones showed different demand patterns. East Harlem South recorded 28.9% of its trips during weekday afternoons, compared with 12.5% in East Harlem North.

The anomaly analysis identified several unusual transactions, including a 0.78-mile trip lasting over 11 hours with a $473 charge, repeated $301 charges on near-zero-distance journeys, and a trip with a calculated average speed of 168 mph.

These records warrant investigation, although some flagged long-distance journeys may be legitimate.

## Strategic Recommendations

Taxi operators should investigate opportunities to maintain driver coverage in East Harlem North while increasing capacity in East Harlem South and Central Park during weekday afternoon peaks.

Management should introduce daily exception reporting to identify unusual fares, durations and speeds, with high-value transactions prioritised for human review.

## Data Limitations

The dataset covers NYC green taxis and records completed journeys rather than unmet passenger demand.

Driver availability, passenger waiting times and operating costs are unavailable, so recorded revenue cannot be interpreted as profit.

Anomaly thresholds are illustrative, and flagged trips do not establish fraud or misconduct.
